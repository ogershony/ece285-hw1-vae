\begin{abstract}
We implement a convolutional variational autoencoder (VAE) in PyTorch and train it on the
pneumonia subset of the Kaggle chest X-ray dataset. We report the hyperparameters, the training
loss curve, Inception Score (IS) and Fr\'echet Inception Distance (FID) of the generated images
computed with PyTorch-Ignite, and qualitative samples. The model reaches an FID of \FID{} and an
IS of \IS{} against the test set.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Model.}
A VAE~\cite{kingma2014} learns an encoder $q_\phi(z \mid x)$ and a decoder $p_\theta(x \mid z)$
with a standard-normal prior on a latent $z \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$. The encoder is a four-layer
strided convolutional network ($4{\times}4$ kernels, stride 2, padding 1) with channel widths
32--64--128--256, BatchNorm after every convolution except the first, and LeakyReLU(0.2)
activations. It maps a $1{\times}64{\times}64$ image to a $256{\times}4{\times}4$ feature map, which
two linear heads turn into $\mu$ and $\log\sigma^2$. Sampling uses the reparameterisation
$z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$. The decoder mirrors the
encoder: a linear layer to $256{\times}4{\times}4$, then four transposed convolutions
(256--128--64--32--1) with BatchNorm and ReLU, ending in a sigmoid so outputs lie in $[0,1]$.
The full model has 2.96M parameters (1.74M encoder, 1.22M decoder).

\paragraph{Objective.}
We minimise the negative evidence lower bound, summed over pixels and the batch:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\sum_i \mathrm{BCE}(\hat{x}_i, x_i)}_{\text{reconstruction}}
\;+\; \underbrace{-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_j \big(1 + \log\sigma_j^2 - \mu_j^2 - \sigma_j^2\big)}_{\text{KL to } \mathcal{N}(0,I)} .
\end{equation}
BCE is appropriate since inputs are in $[0,1]$ and the decoder is sigmoid-bounded.

\paragraph{Data.}
We use the chest X-ray pneumonia dataset~\cite{kermany2018}. The repository contains the
\textsc{Pneumonia} class only: \NumTrain{} training and \NumTest{} test images. Each image is
converted to a single grey channel, resized to $64{\times}64$ for compute reasons, and scaled to
$[0,1]$. No augmentation is used. All code (\texttt{VAE/}, \texttt{train.py}, \texttt{eval\_model.py},
\texttt{data.py}, \texttt{main.py}, \texttt{train\_gpu.sbatch}) is submitted with this report.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Training loss.}
Figure~\ref{fig:loss} shows the per-image training loss (reconstruction plus KL) over
\NumEpochs{} epochs. The loss falls from \InitLoss{} after the first epoch to \FinalLoss{} at
the end, with a minimum of \MinLoss{} at epoch \MinLossEpoch.

\paragraph{Inception Score and FID.}
Following the PyTorch-Ignite GAN-evaluation recipe, we draw $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, decode
one fake image for every real test image, and update Ignite's \texttt{FID} and
\texttt{InceptionScore} metrics batch by batch. Both metrics use the ImageNet-pretrained
Inception-v3 network, so the single grey channel is replicated to three channels and the
$64{\times}64$ images are bilinearly upsampled to $299{\times}299$ before feature extraction.
Table~\ref{tab:metrics} reports the scores. The IS is near its lower bound of 1, as expected:
ImageNet Inception assigns nearly the same class distribution to every chest X-ray, real or
generated. FID, which compares feature statistics of generated and real images directly, is the
more informative number here.

\paragraph{Generated images.}
Figure~\ref{fig:samples} shows sixteen random samples. The model captures the global anatomy
of a frontal chest radiograph: the dark lung fields, the bright mediastinum and spine, and the
shoulder and rib outline. As is typical of a VAE trained with a pixel-wise likelihood, the
samples are blurry and lack fine detail such as individual ribs or vessel markings. Higher
resolution, a perceptual reconstruction term, or a tempered KL weight are natural next steps.
